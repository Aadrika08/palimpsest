\begin{abstract}
AI-driven experiments on interactive simulators need evidence of which interventions
became authoritative, which state was restored, and which computation replay executed.
We implement a semantic contract joining applied-command receipts, controller-visible
observations, continuation state, replay reports and publication status, and evaluate it
on one host with CPU execution: \FaultDeclaredUnits{} controlled challenges, ten continuation pairs, five replay
trajectories and ten paired recording-cost sets. Complete continuation agreed on
\ContinuationPassed{}/\ContinuationDeclared{} pairs and declared replay on
\ReplayPassed{}/\ReplayDeclared{} trajectories, while pose-only restoration changed the
first action on all three ablation seeds; a preregistered component ablation traces that change to stale recurrent
controller state. Median recording overhead versus a null writer was
\SynchronousOverheadMedian{}\% (synchronous), \BackgroundOverheadMedian{}\% (background) and
\StorageOverheadMedian{}\% with storage accounting (worst pair \StorageOverheadMaximum{}\%).
We machine-check in Lean~4 finite-candidate fixed-horizon and anytime bounds for auditing a
controller change and price them at measured cost: the one-hour audit allocation affords
\AuditLaneScenarios{} scenarios, a vacuous radius. We also report, as a negative result, that a
matched-budget navigation development gate failed (\MaleVfourAugGoals{} of \MaleVfourEpisodes{} augmented episodes reached the goal),
so no capability claim is made. One declared clean-checkout unit remains incomplete.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An experimenter adds an obstacle while a recurrent controller navigates a simulated world. The
browser can preview the change while the server accepts it for application at a later control
boundary, and a saved trajectory later plays without error. Was the intervention applied before
a given action, and did replay reproduce its computation? Request acceptance and visual similarity
cannot answer either question. This hypothetical error motivates controlled injections, not a
measured account of user behavior.

Existing systems already separate important parts of the problem: robomimic provides observation
visualization, simulator-state playback and open-loop action playback, and ManiSkill distinguishes action
replay from state-based playback \citep{robomimicplayback,maniskillreplay}; MuJoCo documents the state
needed for continuation \citep{mujococomputation}. We wrap a pinned MuJoCo/FLYNN path
\citep{wang2026flynn} to connect these boundaries: an intervention record identifies application, a
snapshot binds continuation state, a replay report names the executed computation and comparison, and
publication status determines which records a reader may admit. Contributions: (1) a semantic contract with
explicit failure boundaries; (2) an implementation (boundary receipts, observation projection, complete
snapshots, independent branch owners, staged publication); (3) a predeclared evaluation of conformance,
ablation, paired agreement and cost; (4) a machine-checked finite-candidate improvement audit with an exact-rational
bridge and its measured cost (RQ5), plus an honest account of capability studies that did not qualify. Each component has at least
partial precedent (Related Work); the claim is their joint contract and the measured evidence, not any single mechanism.

\section{Semantic Contract}
Write the continuation state as $z_t=(x_t,h_t,w_t,r_t,q_t)$: physics $x_t$, recurrent controller $h_t$,
wrapper $w_t$, random generator $r_t$, pending commands $q_t$. At a boundary the owner processes eligible
commands, $\bar z_t=C(z_t)$, projects the permitted input $o_t=P(\bar z_t)$ and computes
$(a_t,h_{t+1},r'_t)=\pi(o_t,\bar h_t,\bar r_t)$, $z_{t+1}=F(\bar z_t,a_t,h_{t+1},r'_t)$.
Commitment precedes policy execution; a browser display has no authority to substitute for $C$ or $P$.
Acceptance acknowledges queue admission, an applied receipt names its actual control tick and revision,
and rejection and failure are distinct outcomes. Delay is permitted, duplicate application is not.
Equal $qpos$/$qvel$ need not imply equal continuation (hidden state, counters, RNG or pending commands can
differ), and branch independence requires that mutating a child leave parent and siblings unchanged.
Replay has two orthogonal dimensions: what executes (playback, action replay, policy replay) and what is compared.

\textbf{I1: application has an authority boundary.} One owner mutates live state; each application has one
receipt and control tick; expected revisions are checked and duplicates cannot apply twice.
\textbf{I2: continuation binds transition-relevant state and identity.} Restore requires compatible schema,
source/asset identity, and physics, controller, wrapper, random and pending state; incompatible components are
refused before mutating the destination. Equal initial pose is insufficient.
\textbf{I3: evidence names its computation and publication status.} Every replay declares its mode, whether
inference executed, comparison fields and tolerances, and agreement or first divergence; readers distinguish a
validated published prefix from terminal completion. This is a bounded integration contract, not a compositional
theorem. Faults covered: admission/application timing, stale or duplicate commands, incomplete state,
source/asset drift, shared branches, publication interruption and resource failure; the host OS and experiment
code are trusted.

\section{Implementation}
Commands enter a bounded queue; the owner revalidates previewed geometry at application, mutates the world,
refreshes the observation, increments the revision and emits the receipt, so a stale preview cannot authorize
mutation against a new revision. Observations are projected from the authoritative world and kept separate from
world truth and display metadata, locating whether disagreement begins in observation, controller or dynamics.
Snapshots combine physics and mutable world state with controller hidden state, wrapper counters, RNG state, recorder
position and pending commands, bound by source and asset fingerprints (a different checkpoint can load while changing
the transition function). Each branch restores into a separate owner and run. Executable replay checks compatibility,
creates a new destination, restores state and advances the declared computation; action replay bypasses inference and
policy replay executes it, and reports retain mode, execution, agreement and first divergent field and tick.
The recorder writes typed chunks and ordered events under bounded policies with no-overwrite publication, so an
interruption leaves a validated prefix and an ignored unpublished tail without a terminal-success claim.

\section{Evaluation}
The protocol was committed and frozen, not independently preregistered. Seed 0 appeared in disclosed development
checks and fixed seeds are engineering repetitions, not held-out evaluation. Scope is one host (Linux x86-64,
Ryzen 7 7735HS), pinned CPU FLYNN/MuJoCo, same version and architecture. The protocol declares \StudyDeclaredUnits{} descriptors; all
\StudyDeclaredUnits{} are accounted for, \StudyCompletedUnits{} completed, and one clean-checkout unit was not
admitted under its aggregate output reservation and is retained as incomplete. A first campaign completed 69/\StudyDeclaredUnits{}; a
missing benchmark reset (initially misclassified as a resource failure) led to a disclosed amendment, with both
attempts hash-retained. Float32 arrays use absolute and relative tolerance $10^{-6}$ and must be finite; identities
match exactly.

\textbf{RQ1: conformance and ablations.} The \FaultDeclaredUnits{} units cover delayed, stale and duplicate requests, replay mislabeling,
incomplete snapshots, source/asset substitution, custody and benign churn, interrupted publication, shared-owner
branches, and quota, queue and writer failures. Result counters: \FaultCompletedUnits{}/\FaultDeclaredUnits{}
completed units, \FaultBaselineKnownClaims{} claims with known truth and \FaultBaselineUnknownClaims{} unknown,
\FaultBaselineFalseAccepts{} measured false acceptances under weak predicates, \FaultSystemSilentAccepts{} silent
system acceptances and \FaultBenignFalseRejects{} benign false rejections. Unknown truth is not a measured false claim,
and an unrelated crash is not semantic success.

\textbf{RQ2: continuation and replay.} Ten seeds run an eight-tick prefix and a 100-tick source/restored comparison:
agreement \ContinuationPassed{}/\ContinuationDeclared{}. Five source trajectories each run branch, playback, action
replay and policy replay conditions: \ReplayPassed{}/\ReplayDeclared{}. Pose-only restoration, which rewinds the full MuJoCo integration state $x$ but leaves the recurrent controller $h$, wrapper $w$ (environment fields and engine counters),
RNG $r$ and the cached observation at their post-step values (the command queue $q$ is empty at that boundary), changed the first action on all three seeds.
Restore completeness is a documented concern for physics state \citep{mujococomputation}. A preregistered follow-up
(one attempt, \CompCPU{} CPU-s) restored \CompArms{} hybrid snapshots per seed on \CompSeeds{} development seeds through
the production restore path, taking each component from the snapshot or from the post-step state, and compared
\CompHorizon{} ticks. The all-snapshot hybrid agreed on \CompValidSeeds{}/\CompSeeds{} seeds. The first action changed only
when recurrent controller state was stale (\CompHiddenNecessary{}/\CompSeeds{}; other components \CompOtherFirstAction{}),
and restoring it alone on top of physics repaired the first action (\CompHiddenRepairs{}/\CompSeeds{}), contrary to our
preregistered expectation that no single component would. Stale observation, physics and retina state first changed the
action at restored ticks \CompObservationTick{}, \CompPhysicsTick{} and \CompRetinaTick{}; stale wrapper fields and
application state left the recorded tick state unchanged for \CompHorizon{} ticks (\CompWrapperClean{}/\CompSeeds{} and
\CompApplicationClean{}/\CompSeeds{}); no step consumed RNG state, so its restoration was not exercised. Restoring physics,
observation, hidden and retina state removed all divergence (\CompInputsClean{}/\CompSeeds{}). Hybrids pass per-component
validation but need not be states the live engine could reach; the result is specific to this controller and task.
For seed 0 a retained join links the goal-edit receipt (tick 0, revision 1), a tick-8 snapshot holding that receipt and
a digest of 30 events, a separate branch matching 92 ticks, and action and policy replay reports on the same source with
policy execution false and true.

\section{RQ5: Can a Verified Improvement Audit Be Afforded?}
Replay shows what a changed controller computed, not whether it helps. Each observation is one scenario; for $K$
predeclared candidates the contrast is candidate minus baseline loss in $[-1,1]$. A standalone package of nine Lean~4.31
modules pinned to one mathlib revision \citep{demoura2021lean4,mathlib2020} proves two one-sided bounds under explicit
fresh-sampling premises (adapted, independent-of-past, centred increments). At a fixed horizon $n$, some candidate's mean
contrast falls below its expectation by more than $\sqrt{2\log(K/\alpha)/n}$ with probability at most $\alpha$
\citep{hoeffding1963probability}; for a fixed scale $\lambda$, a Ville-type argument
\citep{ville1939etude,howard2021timeuniform} gives the same guarantee at every prefix with radius
$\log(K/\alpha)/(\lambda n)+\lambda/2$, whose floor does not vanish. Exact rational Taylor sums certify an upper bound on
$\log(K/\alpha)$ so a checker can decide the anytime gate in rational arithmetic. All 17 axiom reports list only Lean's
three standard axioms (no \texttt{sorry}); the Ville helper is adapted with attribution from an Apache-2.0 upstream proof \citep{aapbrepo}
and we claim no new probability theorem. The proofs do not verify the parser, the sampler or that a simulator satisfies the
premises.

A four-seed engineering pilot (16 conditions, 1,024 recorded ticks, no retries) measured cost only: no condition recorded
an input-contact tick in 64 ticks, so no loss was defined and nothing is certified. Its supervised tree used \AuditPilotCpu{}
CPU-seconds (\AuditPerScenarioCpu{} per four-condition scenario). With $K=3$ and $\alpha=0.05$ the remaining one-hour
allocation affords \AuditLaneScenarios{} scenarios at radius \AuditLaneRadius{}, vacuous for contrasts in $[-1,1]$; the
whole remaining 24 CPU-hour allocation would afford \AuditTotalScenarios{} scenarios and radius \AuditTotalRadius{}. The
extrapolation assumes the pilot's design including supervisor CPU (\AuditSupervisorShare{}\%) and is neither an upper nor a
lower bound; radii and counts round up.

\section{Related Work}
robomimic and ManiSkill already establish that state and action replay differ \citep{robomimicplayback,maniskillreplay,
mandlekar2022robomimic,tao2025maniskill3,geng2025roboverse}; we claim no priority for that taxonomy, and compare named predicates and
narrow ablations, not these systems. rosbag2, CARLA and Gazebo record at middleware or simulation-state layers
\citep{rosbag2,carlarecorder,gazebologging}; MuJoCo documents integration-state restoration and same-version limits
\citep{mujococomputation}; rr replays user-space processes \citep{ocallahan2017rr}; ReproZip packages environments
\citep{chirigati2013reprozip}. Each addresses a different boundary from declaring what an application applied, restored
and recomputed. Concurrent snapshot, provenance and replay work records world state, hash-chained updates and bit-for-bit replay
\citep{li2026tws,zhou2026simuguard}; in the abstracts and pages we read, we found none that also binds controller hidden state, RNG and pending commands to
an applied-command receipt, but this was an AI-assisted screen that reached only part of the literature, and we claim no priority. Anytime-valid inference
\citep{ville1939etude,howard2021timeuniform} underlies the anytime bound, and Lean 4 work on anytime-valid and finite-audit bounds, including a Lean-checked bound for auditing
post-training changes to a robot model, appeared in 2026
\citep{gupta2026pacbayes,sneiderman2026monitor,sneiderman2026simulated,sneiderman2026fixedn,yang2026formalavs,mittal2026sealed},
alongside Lean libraries and pull requests proving Ville's inequality \citep{lmlexploreville,formalmartingales,atlaslean1250,mathlib45429}.
Our contribution there is a finite-candidate specialization to controller-change audits with an exact-rational bridge and
its measured per-scenario cost and one-hour affordability, not the anytime-valid bound itself.

\section{Negative Results and Limitations}
Claims should match their evidence boundary: admission, application, state agreement, executed computation and
publication are separate facts, tested here through conformance, ablation, agreement and cost, not proved.

\textbf{Capability studies did not qualify.} Predeclared capability studies on the same substrate are unresolved: a
stale-information memory comparison covered 44 of 48 development cells with all 120 applicable correction pairs tied, and
held-out evaluation was not run for it; an earlier recovery-navigation held-out stage did not qualify. A regenerated
matched-budget navigation study (v4) ran its development evaluation once on four fixed scenes and three training seeds,
with the gate fixed in advance at \MaleVfourThreshold{} of \MaleVfourScenes{} scenes per augmented seed. The augmented arm reached the goal
in \MaleVfourAugGoals{} of \MaleVfourEpisodes{} owner episodes (per seed \MaleVfourAugBySeed{}), stalling in \MaleVfourAugStalled{} and timing
out in \MaleVfourAugTimeLimit{} (median final distance \MaleVfourAugMedianDistance{}\,m); the reported-only old-corpus arm
reached it in \MaleVfourOldGoals{} of \MaleVfourEpisodes{} (\MaleVfourOldBySeed{}). The gate was not met, held-out evaluation was not run, and
we make no capability claim. Retained training logs are consistent with underfitting: matched-budget augmented training
loss was roughly 11--12 times the old-corpus loss on all three seeds, and recorded actions were nearly constant. This
comparison is descriptive: training loss is not directly comparable across the two corpora (7,197 vs 2,117 rows), and no
ablation was run, so the cause is not established. A regenerated puck study completed only scene-identifier admission (a first attempt was refused; a second, run under an owner-approved amended protocol, was admitted).

\textbf{Coverage and independence.} One host, one controller and fixed engineering seeds limit coverage; faults do not
exhaust combined faults or estimate field prevalence; no human interpretation or competing-system performance is measured.
Agreement is scoped to CPU, same-version, same-architecture execution; GPU continuation, cross-version portability, parallel
branching and hostile hosts are outside scope. The RQ5 proofs are conditional on fresh-sampling premises that no hash or
replay can establish, and within the audit lane no improvement claim is affordable. Staged publication does not establish
power-loss recovery. Independent-machine reproduction and a public archive with reviewed redistribution terms remain open.

\section{Conclusion}
The contract makes application, continuation, computation and publication claims separately inspectable. Pose-only
restoration diverged immediately, through stale recurrent controller state, while complete continuation agreed in the tested pairs, background recording had a lower
paired median than synchronous writing, and storage-accounted retention exposed long stalls. A machine-checked audit makes
the missing claim precise: certifying improvement needs hundreds to thousands of independent scenarios depending on the target radius, beyond the one-hour lane.
