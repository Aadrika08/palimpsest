## 2. What is finite, and what is self-referential

For a **finite, completely closed, owned process roster**, final OS-accounted CPU is a finite measurement. A closed service and its waited descendants do not require a prediction of their future CPU. `process.py:461–496` uses exact supervisor wait4 usage for the completed subtree; its trusted-job, normal-child-accounting assumptions and drainage refusal are explicit in `process-accounting.md:18–37,53–68,72–101`. Do not double count nested worker receipts.

The helper's caller is different: its self sample occurs at `process.py:483`, before result assembly, return and `finally` cleanup (`:484–525`). Wrapping that caller measures its completed lifetime but creates another caller. If every new receipt writer must both charge its own remaining instructions and certify its own completed exit in that same receipt, no finite chain of wrappers solves the requirement. This is an observation-boundary problem, not evidence that the research computation cannot finish.

The system-manager/kernel accounting model can be a finite endpoint **only if its boundary and delivered measurements are explicitly specified**. Kernel accounting and an already trusted shared OS manager are not another project research finalizer. Every project-specific launcher, client, watchdog, finalizer, audit and report process remains chargeable. Merely renaming one of them “OS administration,” “observer,” or “operator tool” does not put it outside the roster.

A trustworthy closed-unit measurement can include startup, output writes and exit of all processes in that owned unit. It cannot include genuinely outside project processes by assertion. The retained manager source has a real completeness obligation: caching can precede trimming, so complete drain must precede the authoritative cache, not merely eventual path removal (`source-lifetime-static-2026-11-06/README.md:34–52`). Stable readbacks alone cannot settle that. Existing wait4/drain evidence helps, but a wrapper outside that measured subtree is still a distinct tail.

It is unnecessary for the last project-generated JSON to contain its own future cost. A ledger may reference final OS records generated after a process exits, with research audit/publication processes charged in their own subsequent closed entries. However, this terminates only at a specified trusted OS evidence interface, **not** by silently dropping the final bespoke audit/reader. The retained sources do not yet demonstrate a complete such interface for the outside roster. This review therefore proposes that interface, not a claim that it is available today.

## 3. Unsupported premises to remove from admission reasoning

1. **Polling reserve is an absolute bound.** The already rejected 198.4-second arithmetic uses a nominal poll interval, task-count limit and cleanup-attempt duration as lifetime/aggregate-CPU bounds. The helper expressly permits scheduling/probe delays and unresolved drain (`lifetime-architecture.md:15–22`; `process-accounting.md:93–101`). Keep it only as planning headroom; do not revive it.
2. **Hard2 means final CPU ≤2, or hard20 plus an arbitrary 5 means ≤25.** The retained material supplies no universal enforcement/exit allowance. A successful run with an early self sample does not fill that gap. Exit, signal and kernel-accounted work after the threshold must not be assumed free. No new numeric tolerance is derived here.
3. **A conservative budget debit is automatically a measured or proved upper total.** Reserving a generously chosen number protects planning capacity; it does not prove actual usage stayed below it. A policy charge needs its own semantics and approval. It cannot populate `charged_upper_cpu_seconds` or `budget_compliant=true` under the current architecture unless the stated upper-bound condition is actually established.
4. **Two equal cached counters prove the final tail.** They can be stable yet exclude CPU after the cache or outside the unit. Same invocation, ownership, drain-before-final-accounting, no escape/migration and known counter semantics remain substantive prerequisites.
5. **Live mapped-manager attestation proves accounting correctness, or lack of it proves accounting impossible.** Neither follows. A binary hash does not prove kernel scheduling, usage accuracy, no escape or correct application of a trusted OS contract. Conversely, ordinary process-CPU measurement can be explicitly conditional on a trusted deployed OS without claiming full runtime-source attestation.

## 4. Smallest concrete source proposal

Write a short successor **accounting contract**, not another meter/service or probe. Preserve the existing measured service and trusted helper. Name the finite research-process roster and one final measurement source for each role, distinguishing lifetime measurements from partial samples and planning reserves. Use this equation only when its inputs are complete and disjoint:

`closed_research_CPU = closed_owned_service_CPU + sum(final_CPU_of_each_outside_project_process)`

Include pre-interpreter exec prefixes, every client/watchdog, preparation, publication, failure and teardown. Each outside term must be a *closed-process* OS receipt from an already named trusted accounting endpoint, not that process's pre-exit self sample or an asserted RLIMIT value. If a process is already included in the service counter, omit its separate term to avoid double counting. Freeze identity, lifecycle, no-escape and completeness requirements for these measurements; preserve partial/unknown records. This source proposal does not introduce a new platform service to make the observer disappear.

The contract should choose and disclose the following assurance, without conflating its fields:

- **Recommended operational target:** final, complete OS-accounted research CPU under an explicitly trusted OS model; conservative *planning* headroom and practical limit-triggered stopping; acceptance only after every named research role is closed/measured and the actual registered ledger is within the unchanged stage/lane/project caps. Overrun or unresolved usage stops the stage, is retained/charged honestly, and grants no retry or borrowing. Do not claim a prelaunch mathematical never-exceed guarantee. The available retained source does not yet provide all outside final measurements, so this is a concrete conditional design, not READY admission.
- **If the outside endpoint cannot provide final lifetime measurements:** keep those actual fields null and the current stage blocked. A fixed administration **policy debit** is an alternative only after an explicit prospective decision defines it as a budget convention, its exact included roster, and its failure/reconciliation rules. It accounts for overhead by charging it rather than exempting it, but it is *not evidence of the actual aggregate cap*. Retain measured subtotal, unknown actual total and unproved certified-upper fields separately. Do not silently use that convention to claim the original actual-CPU requirement was proved.
- **If strict never-exceed assurance is required:** demand a documented enforcement/accounting model that bounds the complete service and every outside role, including exit. Neither current polling nor source-03 supplies it. No invented allowance, policy debit, successful toy observation or arbitrary reserve closes that proof. Stay blocked without declaring the full project impossible.

In particular, “measure a known process plus a documented conservative charge” is finite **as arithmetic**. It is sufficient for actual-budget acceptance only when the charge is justified as an upper bound in the accepted measurement model, or when the decision maker explicitly adopts a different policy-debit acceptance criterion. Merely documenting a number does not make it conservative in the required evidentiary sense.

This proposal removes the requirement for one self-certifying whole-operation receipt, not any research cost. The finite ledger can have separate close evidence and acceptance records; all project processes used to produce either still need measurement. Any final acceptance reader implemented as a bespoke research process is included, while its last measurement comes from the named trusted OS endpoint. If no such endpoint is identified, say so and do not solve it by excluding that reader. No claim is made that the present tools expose the needed full endpoint.

## 5. OS trust and reproducibility are distinct claims

A bounded technical clarification can state: “CPU and closure receipts are conditional on the trusted kernel/system-manager accounting interface and normal trusted-job process semantics; hostile/compromised OS behavior is outside this assurance.” The current helper already has an explicit trusted-job model. This leaves all project processes charged and does not change the scientific cohort or CPU limits.

Exact package/source correspondence can support why a manager interface is expected to behave as described. It does not reproduce the build, authenticate every live mapping, or itself prove last-tail coverage. The static report correctly separates these (`:18–30`). If the successor claims only OS-accounted CPU under that declared trusted model, full live binary/library-map attestation need not be a logically necessary accounting gate; retain it as unverified reproducibility provenance rather than making ordinary CPU accounting contingent on an ever-expanding platform census.

Changing the currently written exact-platform qualification gate must still be explicit in the successor contract and independently reviewed; do not bypass it in the existing packet or retroactively mark it passed. If the intended claim is exact runtime build/source equivalence, or the threat model includes a substituted manager, live identity remains material and cannot be waved away. It is a different assurance question from the finalizer's CPU tail.
